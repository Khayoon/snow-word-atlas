SNOW — A WORD ATLAS

Double-click "Open Snow Atlas.cmd" to open the interactive atlas.
Node.js is required. There is no npm install or build step.
The local server listens only on your computer (127.0.0.1).
Keep its window open while using the atlas; close it to stop.

EXPLORE
- Click a word or language area to inspect its ancestry and source links.
- Search by language, native word, transcription or ISO language ID.
- Use the language dropdown to reach small areas and lexicon-only entries.
- Scroll to zoom; drag to pan; use reset view to see the full region.
- Toggle word labels, country outlines, and overlapping fills.
- Click an origin set in the legend to focus it. Click again to clear.
- Switch to language branches to compare family relationships.
- Play the guided journey for the PIE, Germanic, Latin and other routes.
- Use the download button to save the current map as an editable SVG.

WHAT IS INCLUDED
80 selected lexical entries and 17 word-origin sets, with modern forms,
selected historical stages, reconstruction notes and dictionary links.
Coverage focuses on Europe, North Africa, the Caucasus and western Central
Asia. Some languages have lexical entries without a matching polygon.
Languages and synonyms are selected, not exhaustive.

GEOGRAPHY AND HONEST LIMITS
Language areas come from published, digitized language atlases through
Glottography. They cross country borders and retain available disjoint
enclaves. They are generalized source-era footprints, not a 2026 language
census. They omit some small communities and multilingual cities.
Overlap does not measure speaker proportions. A filled area is not a claim
of exclusive use, political sovereignty, or uniform language use.
Neutral areas have no mapped lexical match; they are not uninhabited.
Arabic has a standard-reference lexical form over mapped Arabic varieties,
with separate vernacular entries where supplied. See each entry's notes.

WORD HISTORY
Colour means a shared word-origin set, not merely a shared language family.
Greek and Armenian have a different PIE snow/winter root from English snow.
Romanian zapada (zăpadă) is of Slavic origin; the alternative nea follows
the Latin snow line. Hungarian ho (hó) and Finnish lumi belong to different
Uralic word sets. Faroese kavi is separate from poetic snjogvur (snjógvur).
An asterisk marks a reconstructed form. Routes are schematic and omit some
stages; they do not represent dated human migrations. Some deeper origins
remain unresolved. The material is an attributed educational compilation.

EDITING AND REUSE
dist/data/lexicon.json: words, ancestry chains, roots, notes and references.
dist/data/territories.geojson: language polygons and source-specific notes.
dist/data/geometry-sources.json: provenance, licenses and transformations.
dist/data/countries.geojson: Natural Earth context geometry.
dist/app.js and styles.css: interactive map logic and appearance.

All libraries and map files are local. Internet access is needed only to
follow dictionary and provenance links, or to use the hosted edition.
